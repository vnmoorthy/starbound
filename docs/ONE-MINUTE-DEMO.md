# One-minute StarBound demo

Record the website at a desktop resolution. Reset to baseline before recording. Use the recorded Astra experiment so the take does not depend on inference latency. The narration below is about 120 words; leave brief pauses for the visual changes. This is a script, not a generated video file.

| Time | Show | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8 s | Mission control; rotate the 3D swarm slightly | “What would it take to build a civilization powered by a star? This is StarBound: a working Dyson swarm laboratory.” |
| 8–18 s | Robot control, then Manufacturing; briefly change availability | “Start on Mercury. Control robotic excavation, refining and manufacturing. Every change flows into the same material and energy ledger.” |
| 18–29 s | Collector control; show the thermal-failure preset, then reset | “Launch collectors toward solar orbit. Push them too close without enough cooling, and the model rejects their electrical output.” |
| 29–39 s | Earth power; switch optical relay to direct microwave, then restore | “Getting energy home is harder than collecting it. Change the transmission design and watch delivered power collapse under diffraction.” |
| 39–51 s | Astra lab; open the recorded experiment and apply its policy | “Astra proposes a policy. An independent engine tests it. Here, it reaches our power target with far less mining.” |
| 51–60 s | Civilization, then return to the hero or repository | “The equations, assumptions and model comparison are public. StarBound turns a cosmic ambition into engineering decisions we can inspect.” |

## Recording notes

- Let numbers settle after each control change. Avoid running live inference inside a 60-second recording.
- Keep the cursor deliberate; cut between tabs if navigation consumes the narration time.
- Call the Astra sequence a recorded experiment. Do not imply live generation if you show replay.
- The fair comparison is a tie on the measured mining objective. If asked, say so directly.
- Photorealistic factory images are labeled concepts; the moving swarm is a representative visualization, not a collision-resolved orbital simulation.
